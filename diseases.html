Eye Diseases We Treat

Our practice specializes in diagnosing and treating a comprehensive range of eye conditions. Below are some of the common eye diseases we manage with expertise and care.

Cataracts

Cataracts occur when the natural lens of the eye becomes cloudy, leading to blurred vision, glare, and difficulty seeing in low light. This is one of the most common age-related eye conditions.

Symptoms:

  • Cloudy or blurry vision
  • Increased sensitivity to glare and light
  • Difficulty seeing at night
  • Fading or yellowing of colors
  • Frequent changes in eyeglass prescription

Treatment: Cataract surgery is a safe and effective procedure that replaces the cloudy lens with a clear artificial lens, restoring clear vision.

Glaucoma

Glaucoma is a group of eye diseases that damage the optic nerve, often due to increased pressure within the eye. It is a leading cause of irreversible blindness but can be managed effectively with early detection and treatment.

Symptoms:

  • Often no early symptoms (open-angle glaucoma)
  • Gradual loss of peripheral vision
  • Tunnel vision in advanced stages
  • Severe eye pain and nausea (acute angle-closure glaucoma)
  • Blurred vision and halos around lights

Treatment: Treatment includes prescription eye drops, laser therapy, or surgery to lower eye pressure and prevent further damage to the optic nerve.

Age-Related Macular Degeneration (AMD)

AMD affects the macula, the central part of the retina responsible for sharp, detailed vision. It is a leading cause of vision loss in people over 50 and can make reading and recognizing faces difficult.

Symptoms:

  • Blurred or reduced central vision
  • Difficulty reading or seeing fine details
  • Dark or empty areas in central vision
  • Straight lines appearing wavy or distorted
  • Decreased intensity or brightness of colors

Treatment: Treatment depends on the type (dry or wet AMD) and may include nutritional supplements, anti-VEGF injections, laser therapy, or photodynamic therapy.

Diabetic Retinopathy

Diabetic retinopathy is a complication of diabetes that affects blood vessels in the retina. It can lead to vision loss if not detected and treated early. Regular eye exams are crucial for diabetic patients.

Symptoms:

  • Often no symptoms in early stages
  • Floaters or dark spots in vision
  • Blurred or fluctuating vision
  • Difficulty with color perception
  • Vision loss in advanced stages

Treatment: Treatment includes blood sugar control, laser photocoagulation, anti-VEGF injections, or vitrectomy surgery in advanced cases.

Dry Eye Syndrome

Dry eye syndrome occurs when your eyes don't produce enough tears or when tears evaporate too quickly. This common condition can cause discomfort and affect vision quality.

Symptoms:

  • Stinging or burning sensation in the eyes
  • Feeling of something in your eyes
  • Redness and irritation
  • Excessive tearing (reflex response to dryness)
  • Blurred vision and eye fatigue

Treatment: Treatment includes artificial tears, prescription medications, punctal plugs, lifestyle modifications, and treatment of underlying causes.

Retinal Detachment

Retinal detachment is a serious emergency where the retina separates from the back of the eye. Immediate medical attention is required to prevent permanent vision loss.

Symptoms:

  • Sudden appearance of floaters
  • Flashes of light in peripheral vision
  • Shadow or curtain over part of visual field
  • Sudden decrease in vision

Treatment: Surgical repair is necessary and may include laser surgery, cryotherapy, pneumatic retinopexy, or vitrectomy.

Conjunctivitis (Pink Eye)

Conjunctivitis is an inflammation of the conjunctiva, the thin membrane covering the white part of the eye. It can be caused by infections, allergies, or irritants.

Symptoms:

  • Redness in the white of the eye
  • Itching or burning sensation
  • Discharge (watery or thick)
  • Crusting of eyelids
  • Increased tear production

Treatment: Treatment depends on the cause and may include antibiotic drops (bacterial), antihistamines (allergic), or supportive care (viral).

Corneal Diseases

The cornea is the clear front surface of the eye. Various conditions can affect the cornea, including infections (keratitis), dystrophies, and injuries.

Symptoms:

  • Eye pain and redness
  • Blurred or hazy vision
  • Sensitivity to light
  • Tearing and discharge
  • Feeling of something in the eye

Treatment: Treatment varies based on the condition and may include medications, specialty contact lenses, or corneal transplant surgery.
